## What's new

- **LAN access.** Open the app from your phone, tablet or another computer in the same network. Off by default: switch it on in Settings → LAN access and restart the program. This computer works as before; every other device logs in once with the token. After 5 wrong tokens a device has to wait a minute, and AI tools (MCP) still only connect from this computer. Use it only on networks you trust, such as your home Wi-Fi: the connection is not encrypted.
- **Log in with a QR code.** Settings → LAN access → *Show QR code*, then scan it with the camera of the phone or tablet. The code works once and only for 5 minutes, so a photo or screenshot of it is useless afterwards.
- **Calendar subscription.** Settings → Calendar subscription (off by default) gives you a link that Apple Calendar, Outlook or Thunderbird subscribe to. They refresh it by themselves, so your dates show up there without copies. *New link* makes the old link stop working. In Apple Calendar on a Mac, choose *On My Mac* as the location, not iCloud.
- **Install as an app.** In Chrome or Edge, the Install icon in the address bar puts the app into its own window with a start menu or dock icon. The app also has a tab icon now.
- **Pace forecast.** Word or page goals now also show your speed so far and when you will be done at that speed: "At your speed so far, 300 words per day since 2 Nov: done on 4 Dec, 4 days after the deadline". It turns red when that is after the deadline, and it also works without a deadline. The tree adds "done ~4 Dec".
- **Tags look like tags.** The tag filters below the search have their own tinted style with a thin border, so they no longer look like the buttons next to the search. The tag list wraps instead of being cut off.
- **Import recognises its own events.** Calendar events exported by this app carry the entry id and are matched by it on import, even after you renamed or moved the entry.

## Fixes

- Windows and WSL could both open the same data folder at the same time, because each thought the other's lock was left over from a crash. The lock now records the operating system and is never taken over across systems.
